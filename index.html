Inquiry-based teaching in American history, civics, and geography for fifth-grade classrooms.

01 / About

A teacher-leader model for inquiry-based instruction.

American Roots is a UCI Teacher Academy initiative proposed to strengthen fifth-grade teaching in American history, civics, and geography. It connects the Declaration of Independence, the Constitution, and the Bill of Rights to the historical questions and local places students can investigate.

Teachers study with historians and instructional experts, develop and pilot primary-source units, and refine their teaching through student work and collaboration. Disciplinary reading, discussion, and evidence-based writing are central to the approach, with supports that help English Learners access demanding texts.

Four goals guide the work
  1. Deepen teachers’ knowledge of founding principles and documents.
  2. Build teachers’ capacity to direct and evaluate AI as a support for curriculum design.
  3. Develop six inquiry-based, primary-source units for fifth-grade classrooms.
  4. Build a sustained teacher-leader network for implementation, reflection, and improvement.

02 / American Roots

The proposed learning cycle combines content study, curriculum development, summer institutes, and academic-year support.

Proposed project period
October 1, 2026–September 30, 2029

01

Teacher leadership & unit design

Teacher Leader Writers collaborate with project staff to author units. Curriculum Pilot Teachers test them in fifth-grade classrooms. Together, they review student work and revise materials before sharing them more widely.

02

Summer institutes & classroom support

The narrative proposes three place-based summer institutes, each serving 30 Teacher Practitioners. Participants experience the units as learners, plan classroom implementation, and return during the school year to discuss teaching and analyze student work.

03

Three years of inquiry

Year 1: Declaration of Independence.
Year 2: Constitution and Bill of Rights.
Year 3: Local and national history and geography through place.

Two units are planned each year. Place-based study connects Southern California communities with Philadelphia, Washington, D.C., and Maryland’s Eastern Shore.

04

Human-first AI

Teachers use AI as a guided thinking partner to design, adapt, and refine materials. Teachers remain responsible for verifying historical claims, reviewing outputs, and making instructional decisions before classroom use.

03 / Resources

Six units. A shared approach.

Materials designed for teachers to use, adapt, and share.

The narrative proposes open-access instructional units, facilitator guides, student-facing resources, assessments, and teacher-guided AI models and prompts for voluntary national use.

Each unit follows CLIC: Content, Literacy, Inquiry, and Citizenship, connecting historical knowledge with close reading, compelling questions, primary-source evidence, and informed civic participation.

What will the instructional units include?

Units are planned to combine founding documents, maps, literature, and other primary sources. Students investigate essential questions and develop evidence-based arguments. Classroom piloting and student-work review will inform revisions before release.

Are classroom resources available now?

American Roots units and facilitator materials have not been posted on this website yet. The grant narrative (PDF, opens in a new tab) describes the planned materials and their development.

How will the project study its progress?

The proposed evaluation combines teacher content-knowledge assessments, curriculum reviews, student-work analysis, implementation documentation, and teacher reflections. Findings are intended to guide improvements across each annual cycle.
